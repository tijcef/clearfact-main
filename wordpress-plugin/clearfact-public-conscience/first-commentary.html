The Public Conscience | Opinion / Commentary

By Emmanuel Sunday Tijwun
Public Affairs and Humanitarian Affairs Commentator

Nigeria must work for Nigerians. That should be the first test of leadership: whether ordinary people can live with dignity, earn an honest living and look to the future with hope.

Public office is a responsibility to serve. It should never become a shelter from the difficulties citizens face. A government's achievements must mean something beyond speeches, ceremonies and photographs. They must reach homes, classrooms, hospitals and communities.

Human dignity must guide public policy

Behind every policy is a person. A transport decision affects the worker trying to reach a job. An education decision affects a student trying to finish school. A healthcare decision affects a family deciding how to pay for treatment. Leaders should explain what their choices mean for those people, especially those with the least power to absorb the consequences.

When citizens raise concerns about essential services, they deserve clear answers. They should know what government intends to do, how public money will be used and when promised improvements can reasonably be expected.

Accountability must become a habit

Accountability should continue after elections. Citizens should be able to examine budgets, ask questions about projects and compare promises with delivery. Public officials should welcome scrutiny as part of the work they accepted.

We should ask practical questions. What problem does a project solve? Who benefits? What does it cost? Who is responsible for delivering it? How will the public know whether it succeeded? These questions can move debate from loyalty to results.

Citizens also have a responsibility

A stronger Nigeria requires citizens who insist on evidence, reject misinformation and apply the same standards to every leader. Wrongdoing should not become acceptable because the person responsible shares our party, religion or ethnic identity.

Public criticism can be firm without becoming hateful. We can challenge a policy, demand an explanation and defend a community without spreading unverified claims or attacking people's humanity.

The measure of leadership

My position is straightforward: government should be judged by how well it serves the public. Leadership should protect people, make institutions more dependable and create conditions in which effort has a fair chance of producing opportunity.

The Public Conscience will be a space to examine these responsibilities and speak for human dignity. Its purpose is to ask questions that matter to citizens and encourage public debate rooted in fairness and accountability.

Nigeria belongs to its people. Those entrusted with power must remember that service is the purpose of their office. Nigerians deserve a country that works for them.
